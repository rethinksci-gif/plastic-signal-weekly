Aduro Clean Technologies reported an 88.8% to 90.8% condensable hydrocarbon yield across changing polyolefin feeds during a 75-hour campaign of its NGP \(next-generation process\) chemical recycling route, per The Globe and Mail. The figures are company-reported, early-stage pilot data with no stated comparison baseline, feed specifications, or product quality details.

rss · Future Materials · Sep 23, 12:28

**「Why it matters」** Chemical recycling licensors and polyolefin producers weighing conversion routes track continuous-run yield stability, because a narrow yield band across variable mixed-polyolefin feed would reduce feedstock sorting and blending constraints on the front end of a plant.

**「Practical implication」** For brand owners and converters assessing recycled-content sourcing, this remains directional only: 75 hours is short of commercial continuous operation, and the absence of verified output purity, downstream polymer qualification, and cost per tonne means no specification or offtake decision can rest on it yet.

**「Next action」** Ask Aduro for the campaign&\#x27;s feed composition, product slate and testing method, then compare against the yield and run-length data disclosed by other chemical recycling processes.
