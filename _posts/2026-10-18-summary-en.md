### [Water-processed fluorine-free battery binder shows lab-scale gains](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/10/16/researchers-work-on-fluorine-free-battery-binder/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

A Nature Communications study from UC San Diego and Toyota Research Institute of North America describes a fluorine-free lithium-ion binder: polyacrylic acid and carboxymethyl cellulose dissolved in water and crosslinked with citric acid, replacing fluorinated binders requiring toxic N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone \(NMP\). The lab-stage binder worked with lithium iron phosphate, nickel-rich and silicon electrodes and separated in water for direct recycling. Its lifecycle analysis estimates 6.9–9.3% manufacturing energy savings and 7.1% higher manufacturer profit; recycling shows 10% less energy, 3% less water and 7.2% lower greenhouse gas emissions. Binder cost is undetermined.

rss · Resource Recycling — markets and policy · Oct 16, 13:27

**「Why it matters」** Binder choice fixes the solvent, drying and solvent-recovery steps in electrode production and determines whether coatings can be stripped for direct recycling — shifting qualification work onto slurry, coating and cell-integration teams.

**「Practical implication」** Substitution would require requalifying slurry rheology, adhesion and cycle life per electrode chemistry; the savings figures are modeled from lab-scale data, and no binder price, output volume or manufacturer trial appears in the supplied material.

**「Next action」** Request the study's test conditions and cycle-life data, and ask whether pilot-scale coating trials with industry partners are planned.

**Tags**: `#fluorine-free binder`, `#polyacrylic acid`, `#carboxymethyl cellulose`, `#water-based processing`, `#lab-scale`
